# Taimoor's opening: supplied original draft

Imagine a bank customer who moves some savings into an investment account and buys exchange-traded funds, or ETFs. An ETF holds a collection of investments, such as company shares, while its units trade on a stock exchange.
The bank knows which ETFs the customer owns. But an analyst may also need to understand the industries and countries represented within them.
Providers publish percentages, such as “30 percent technology.” To interpret that figure, we need its observation date, the meaning of the category, and the source supporting it. Otherwise, we could combine outdated values or categories that sound similar but mean different things.
Provider factsheets and product pages already report dated sector and geographic allocations. The Financial Industry Business Ontology, or FIBO, provides a shared financial vocabulary. Earlier work using FIBO, called the Global Fund Reporting Ontology, or GFRO, also demonstrates dated fund information and allocation summaries.
These approaches give us useful building blocks. Shahmeer will now explain the specific gap and our proposed model.
