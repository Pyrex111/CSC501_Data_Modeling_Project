## Decisions and assumptions to apply consistently

- Prototype scope is one bank, individual customers, one ETF fund/share class per ETF record, one sampled provider per ETF, and one primary captured source per allocation snapshot. Corporate customers, joint ownership, provider-role history, trades and risk calculations are outside this implementation.
- CustomerNumber is the bank's business reference, not another identifier generated merely for the application's database. PortfolioReference is a business reference scoped to the owning customer. Sample bank records may be synthetic and must be labeled as such.
- Provider registry values identify the actual sampled legal company and its issuing registration jurisdiction. Use verified values when claiming real companies. Synthetic provider records must be labeled synthetic. Do not invent an identifier for a real provider or substitute a brand name for a legal entity without explanation.
- Attributes marked with an asterisk are optional; all others are required. Trim text inputs and reject empty strings for required business identifiers. Names and email addresses are not unique identifiers.
- IdentifierScheme is restricted to ISIN and Ticker in the initial prototype. An ISIN record has no ExchangeContext; a Ticker record requires one. Apply scheme-specific uniqueness using partial unique indexes or an equally explicit implementation. Do not assume that `UNIQUE(Scheme, Value, NULL)` prevents duplicates in SQLite.
- ClassificationScheme uniqueness applies to (SchemeAuthority, SchemeName, Version) when the version is known. An unresolved version has no asserted complete business key. Keep Id for record identity. Unknown-version records must not be used to establish shared category meaning across disclosures.
- ContextKey is a documented scope token, not evidence by itself. For a verified classification edition, derive it from that authority/scheme/version. Otherwise derive a disclosure-local context from the source URL, retrieval time, allocation location and dimension. Do not put unrelated unknown classifications in a single `Unknown` context.
- AllocationCategory uniqueness is (AllocationDimension, ContextKey, Label), under the explicit prototype assumption that a label occurs once within an allocation context. This assumption concerns record identity inside that context; matching labels across contexts do not establish equivalence. Revisit this rule if a source contains duplicate labels for distinct categories within one context.
- An allocation snapshot is one dated allocation block, identified in scope by its ETF, dimension, allocation date, captured source and SourceLocator. SourceLocator identifies the relevant page/table/section within the capture. Distinct allocation bases require distinct block locators or a revised uniqueness rule.
- Store fractions consistently: 0.30 means 30%. Holding and entry fractions must be between 0 and 1. CoverageStatus is Complete, Partial or Unknown. Validate complete-disclosure totals using an explicitly documented rounding tolerance. Do not demand that a partial disclosure or a partial sample of portfolio holdings sums to 1.
- SourceDocument is a captured version, not merely a live URL. Store RetrievedAt in a single documented UTC representation and retain the captured file at CapturedFileRef. PublishedAt may be unknown. Two snapshots may legitimately refer to different captures of the same URL.
- Every AllocationEntry must use a category whose dimension matches its snapshot. Enforce this on inserts and updates, including later changes to a category or snapshot dimension. A database trigger or suitable relational constraint design should protect this invariant; interface validation alone is insufficient.
- Every completed snapshot has one or more entries. A foreign key alone does not enforce this parent-side minimum. Save the snapshot and entries together in a transaction and validate before completion; do not leave a snapshot empty after deleting its final entry.
- Preserve the distinction between HoldingAsOfDate and AllocationAsOfDate. Display both where portfolio and allocation information are combined. Do not silently treat them as the same observation date.
