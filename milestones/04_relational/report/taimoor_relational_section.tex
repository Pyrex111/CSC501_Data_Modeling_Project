Twelve relations and thirteen foreign keys implement the revised ER model.
\texttt{Holding} represents the dated Portfolio--ETF association;
\texttt{AllocationEntry} associates a disclosure snapshot with a category and
weight. Holding and allocation dates remain independent. Page three lists
all relations, attributes and constraints.

Every table has an autogenerated integer \texttt{Id}. Candidate keys preserve
customer numbers, portfolio references within each customer, provider
registration within a jurisdiction, unique ISINs and tickers within an exchange. Source
captures use URL plus retrieval timestamp. Category uniqueness includes
dimension, context and label: context identifies a known scheme edition or
an unresolved captured block. Matching labels do not establish equivalence.

\texttt{ETFProvider} models registered companies; \texttt{Customer} models
individual bank customers. Names describe these records rather than identify
them. A portfolio belongs to one customer, and its dated holdings refer to
ETFs. Each disclosure groups one dated sector or geography allocation block
from a retained capture. Entries refer to categories rather than repeating
unscoped labels. A known classification stores its authority, name and edition;
unresolved categories remain local to the capture and locator. Thus two
``Technology'' labels need not denote the same investment grouping, even when
their reported percentages happen to agree.

SQLite STRICT tables, scalar checks and foreign keys enforce types, dates,
fractions and references~\cite{sqliteStrict,sqliteForeignKeys}. Four triggers
protect category/snapshot dimensions. The transaction validator checks required
identifiers/entries, contexts and Complete totals within $0.005$ of one.
This configurable fraction tolerance is provisional, not source-calibrated.
Raw SQL can bypass transaction checks. File existence is checked only when
\texttt{capture\_root} is supplied; it does not establish authenticity.
